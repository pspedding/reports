Chart 2 — Net Migration by Visa Category (stacked)

Breakdown of net migration by major visa type. Temporary Student (purple) dominates the post-COVID surge but was growing steadily under LNP from 2013–2018. Negative values in 2020–2021 reflect COVID border closure.

Chart 3 — Student Visa Net: Raw vs COVID-Adjusted

If 2020–2023 are averaged (~106k/yr), the trend shows continuity with the LNP-era growth trajectory — not a post-2022 policy shift. The dashed yellow line shows what would have occurred without border closure.

Chart 4 — Permanent vs Temporary Net Migration

Temporary migration dominates and drives headline volatility. Permanent migration is relatively stable across both governments. Australian citizens (brown) are net-negative most years — more leave than return.

Chart 5 — Average Annual Net Migration by Visa Type: ALP vs LNP

COVID-adjusted averages (ALP: 2007–2012, 2022–2025; LNP: 2006, 2013–2021). Most visa categories show similar or modest differences. The headline gap is driven primarily by student and temporary categories — categories driven by global demand, not policy settings alone.

Chart 7 — Top Source Countries: Student Visa Surge 2006–2025 ✦ New

Surge countries identified by comparing 2021–25 avg vs 2006–10 avg. Nepal, Philippines, Colombia, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Brazil, Kenya all show 2.5×–6.5× increases. Media-cited countries (Somalia, Lebanon) do not appear in ABS student visa data at significant volumes.

Chart 9 — NZ Subclass 444 vs Temporary Student Visa Net Migration 2006–2025 ✦ New

Measurement note: The Subclass 444 (Special Category Visa) is normally granted each time an eligible NZ citizen enters Australia and ceases when the holder leaves — so raw arrival counts can record the same individual repeatedly across multiple trans-Tasman trips. These figures use NOM (12/16-month rule), not raw arrivals. NZ citizen net migration grew strongly post-COVID (37k+ in 2024–25), driven by demand-side recovery with no policy change. The ABS explicitly warns that Overseas Arrivals and Departures border-movement data should not be treated as a measure of population change; Net Overseas Migration is the appropriate measure.

Chart 8 — Media Narrative vs Data Reality: 2021–25 Average Annual Student Visa Arrivals ✦ New

Somalia and Lebanon do not appear in ABS student visa data at measurable volumes. Iran shows moderate growth (+97%) from a small base (538 → 1,060 avg/yr) — not a surge by any standard. The countries most cited in social cohesion and crime discourse are not the countries driving student visa growth. The political narrative is largely disconnected from the visa data.
